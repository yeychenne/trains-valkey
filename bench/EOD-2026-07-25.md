# EOD - 2026-07-25

## Objective

Evaluate whether TRAINS can provide the distributed ordering and replication
plane while Arctic provides a concurrent materialized-state plane for the
Valkey experiment.

## Conclusion

The composition is technically coherent, with a precise division of work:

- TRAINS owns total ordering, deduplication, failure detection, view changes,
  rejoin, and the snapshot boundary.
- Arctic owns local point reads/writes and ordered indexing after an operation
  has been delivered by TRAINS.
- Valkey remains the reference implementation and compatibility oracle during
  the first experiment.

Arctic is not a drop-in acceleration for the current `RedisBackend`. The proxy
holds the store behind `Arc<Mutex<S>>`, `RedisStore::apply` requires `&mut self`,
and the real backend performs a blocking RESP round trip. A direct replacement
would test semantics but would not expose Arctic's concurrent scaling.

Arctic point operations are suitable for the candidate path. Its range and
prefix scans are non-linearizable during concurrent mutation, so they must not
define a state-transfer cut. TRAINS should establish an ordered barrier before
the experiment computes a scan-based snapshot or final state digest.

## Implementation Result

The standalone `experiments/arctic-shadow` crate now implements seven gates:

- direct Valkey/Arctic point-command and snapshot equivalence;
- duplicate delivered-operation suppression;
- healthy three-node convergence under two concurrent issuers;
- passive snapshot import plus delivered-log tail catch-up;
- live crash, reduced-view writes, stale-node restart, promotion/readmission,
  and restored full-view convergence;
- deterministic fault schedules over six seeds and all three crash victims; and
- a 4,096-command seeded Valkey/Arctic differential manifest with an independent
  expected-state model.

The lifecycle test retains the crashed member's stale Valkey process, discards
its in-process Arctic state, and restarts the same logical TRAINS member and
ring address. It then requires:

- writes acknowledged in the reduced two-node view;
- full mirrored snapshot replacement and live tail catch-up;
- a recovered-node-origin write after promotion;
- a survivor-origin write after readmission; and
- identical 56-key Valkey and Arctic snapshots on all three nodes.

This closes the earlier boundary where only a restarted passive process was
modeled while a healthy three-node ring supplied state.

## AWS Comparison And Decision

Aurora DSQL validates the architectural pattern of deterministic post-images,
an ordered replicated stream, independently scalable materialized storage, and
snapshot-plus-log reconstruction. The open experiment is deliberately smaller:
it uses one global TRAINS write order, has no multi-region target, and requires
at least one survivor because its delivered-effect tail is runtime-retained
rather than an external durable Journal.

The seven TRAINS assurance methods remain the protocol-kernel foundation.
Adding Arctic supplies a composition case study against independently developed
open-source code, but does not automatically extend the kernel proof to the
whole proxy. The refinement map, deterministic composition simulation,
real-process lifecycle test, acknowledged-operation manifest, and Valkey
differential oracle form the additional evidence.

Decision: continue through correctness gates 1-5, then make the investment
decision before performance engineering. The service contract, refinement map,
durability boundary, and decision criteria are recorded in
`docs/ADR-002-trains-arctic-assurance-boundary.md`.

All five gates are now complete. The decision is **GO for a scoped performance
exploration in a separate phase**, without yet claiming that Arctic improves
throughput or cross-node read semantics.

## Next Phase

Performance exploration is now permitted but remains a separate phase. It must
first expose Arctic concurrency rather than benchmark the current shared mutex,
then compare unmodified Valkey, `Mutex<BTreeMap>`, and Arctic under identical
single-key workloads. Throughput, p50/p99 latency, CPU, RSS, and scaling by
client/thread count are benchmark outputs, not correctness or CI pass
conditions.

The known correctness boundaries remain unchanged: the five-command subset,
NUL-free keys, local rather than arbitrary cross-node linearizable reads, one
surviving recovery source, and no multi-region claim.

The first local data-plane benchmark is complete. Across three repetitions,
Arctic is approximately tied with `Mutex<BTreeMap>` at one thread and about 12x
faster at eight threads across read-only, 90/10 mixed, and write-only workloads.
This supports a narrow ordered-writer/shared-reader interface prototype, not a
production switch or a distributed throughput claim. Full methodology and
results are in `bench/results/arctic-data-plane-2026-07-25.md`.
